Year-end accounts, filed on time and actually explained.

Every limited company has to file accounts with Companies House and a tax return with HMRC, whatever its size. Sole traders need a set of figures for their Self Assessment. We prepare them, file them, and then sit down with you and go through what they say about the business.

Limited company accounts

Private companies must file accounts at Companies House within nine months of their year end, and the corporation tax return with HMRC within twelve. Miss the Companies House date and the penalty starts at £150 and climbs to £1,500, doubling if you are late two years running. We work backwards from those dates and ask for your records early enough that there is never a rush.

Most of our company clients qualify as micro-entities or small companies, which means a shorter set of accounts under FRS 105 or FRS 102 Section 1A. We prepare the full version for your records and file the abridged version the public gets to see, where the rules allow it. We also handle the confirmation statement and keep the Companies House register up to date when directors, addresses or shareholdings change.

Sole trader and partnership accounts

If you are self-employed there is no legal format for your accounts, but you still need accurate figures for your tax return and for anything else that comes up, such as a mortgage application or a tender. We prepare a proper income and expenditure account, make sure you have claimed everything you are entitled to, and keep the working papers so there are no awkward gaps if HMRC ever asks.

From April 2026, sole traders and landlords with income over £50,000 come into Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, with the threshold dropping to £30,000 in 2027 and £20,000 in 2028. That means quarterly digital updates rather than one annual return, and the accounts process changes with it. We are moving clients onto suitable software now rather than waiting.

What you get back from us

Accounts are not just a filing. Once they are done we go through the headline numbers with you: how profit compares with last year, where the money went, how much tax is coming and when, and whether the business is carrying anything it should not be, like an overdrawn director's loan account. Half an hour on this once a year saves a lot of surprises.

Switching to us mid-year? That is fine. We request the previous accountant's working papers through the standard professional clearance process, and most hand over without fuss. You do not need to wait for your year end.

Common questions

What records do you need from me?

Bank statements for the year, sales invoices, purchase invoices and receipts, details of any loans or finance, and a note of anything unusual. If you use Xero, QuickBooks or FreeAgent and keep it reasonably up to date, most of this is already there and we just need access.

My company has not traded. Do I still need accounts?

Yes. A dormant company still has to file dormant accounts and a confirmation statement each year. They are simple and cheap, but the penalties for forgetting are the same as for a trading company.

Can you do accounts for a year that has already passed?

Yes, including late ones. If you have fallen behind we will get the overdue filings in first to stop penalties growing, then get you onto a normal cycle.

Do you need to visit my premises?

No. Everything can be done by email, phone and shared software, though you are always welcome at the office and some clients prefer to drop records in person.
