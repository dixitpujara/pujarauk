Corporation tax: the return, and the planning around it.

Every company pays corporation tax on its profits, but how much depends on decisions made during the year, not just at the end. We prepare the return and, more usefully, talk to you before the year end while there is still time to do something about it.

The basics

Corporation tax is currently 19% on profits up to £50,000 and 25% on profits over £250,000, with a sliding rate in between called marginal relief. Those thresholds are shared between associated companies, so if you own more than one company the bands shrink. The tax is due nine months and one day after the year end, and the return (the CT600, with full accounts and tax computations attached) is due twelve months after.

Unlike Self Assessment, HMRC does not send a bill. You have to work out what is owed and pay it, which is where most of the trouble starts for companies without an accountant.

Where the savings usually are

  • Capital allowances. Most equipment, vans and computers qualify for 100% relief in the year of purchase through the Annual Investment Allowance. Timing a purchase before rather than after the year end can bring the relief forward twelve months.
  • Salary and dividends. How a director takes money out affects corporation tax, income tax and National Insurance together. We look at the whole picture each year rather than one tax at a time.
  • Pension contributions. Employer contributions are generally an allowable expense and are one of the few ways to move money out of a company efficiently.
  • Losses. If the company has made a loss we will make sure it is carried back or forward in the way that produces the quickest refund or the biggest future saving.
  • R&D relief. Fewer companies qualify than the adverts suggest, but genuine development work in software, engineering or manufacturing can still attract a worthwhile credit. We will give you an honest view.

What we do

We prepare the computation and the CT600, file it with HMRC alongside the accounts in the required iXBRL format, and tell you the amount and payment date well in advance. Before the year end we will have a short planning conversation to see whether anything is worth bringing forward or deferring. If HMRC raises an enquiry, we deal with it.

Common questions

When do I have to pay corporation tax?

Nine months and one day after your accounting year end. For a 31 March year end that is 1 January. Larger companies with profits over £1.5 million pay in quarterly instalments, but that does not apply to most owner-managed businesses.

Is it better to take salary or dividends?

Usually a mix: a salary around the National Insurance threshold to secure a state pension year, with the rest as dividends. The best split changes each tax year as thresholds move, and it also depends on whether you have other income, so we review it annually rather than setting it once.

Can I claim my car through the company?

You can, but for most petrol or diesel cars the benefit-in-kind charge outweighs the saving. Fully electric cars are a different story and are currently very tax-efficient through a company. Vans are treated more generously than cars. Ask before you buy, not after.

My company made a loss. Do I still file a return?

Yes, and it is worth doing properly because the loss can be set against last year’s profit for a refund, or carried forward against future profits.
