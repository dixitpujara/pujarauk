VAT without the quarterly panic.

VAT is the tax most small businesses get wrong, usually by registering late, choosing the wrong scheme, or claiming things they should not. We register you at the right time, pick the scheme that suits how you trade, and file every return from MTD-compatible software.

When you have to register

You must register for VAT once your taxable turnover in any rolling twelve-month period goes over £90,000, or as soon as you expect it to in the next thirty days alone. The rolling part catches people out: it is not your accounting year, it is any twelve months. We keep an eye on this for clients who are approaching the threshold so that registration happens on time and not after a penalty letter.

Some businesses choose to register voluntarily below the threshold, typically because their customers are VAT-registered businesses and the ability to reclaim VAT on costs outweighs the admin. We will tell you if that makes sense for you.

Choosing a scheme

  • Standard accounting — VAT on invoices issued and received. The default, and right for most.
  • Cash accounting — VAT accounted for when money actually moves. Helpful if customers pay slowly.
  • Flat rate scheme — pay a fixed percentage of gross turnover and keep the difference. Simpler, and sometimes cheaper, but the "limited cost trader" rules mean it suits fewer businesses than it used to.
  • Annual accounting — one return a year with payments on account. Less admin, less visibility.

Builders and contractors also need to understand the domestic reverse charge, which changes who accounts for the VAT on most construction work between VAT-registered businesses. We set this up correctly in your software so invoices come out right.

Making Tax Digital

All VAT-registered businesses must keep digital records and file returns through MTD-compatible software; the old online form is gone. We set clients up on Xero, QuickBooks or FreeAgent, connect it to HMRC, and either file from your records or do the bookkeeping ourselves and file from that. Returns are reviewed by a qualified accountant before submission, which is how errors get caught before HMRC catches them.

Already registered and worried about past returns? Errors under £10,000 can usually be corrected on the next return. Larger ones need a separate disclosure, and it is far better to make one voluntarily than to wait for an inspection. We can review your last four years and tell you where you stand.

Common questions

What happens if I registered late?

HMRC will backdate your registration to the date you should have registered, and you owe VAT on sales from then even if you did not charge it. There is also a penalty based on how late you were. We can often reduce the damage by reclaiming VAT on costs for the same period and negotiating the penalty.

Can I reclaim VAT on things I bought before registering?

Yes, within limits: goods you still hold bought up to four years before registration, and services up to six months before. Keep the invoices.

How often do I have to file?

Quarterly for most businesses. Your quarters are set when you register but can be changed to line up with your year end. Payment is due one month and seven days after the quarter ends.

Should I be on the flat rate scheme?

Only if your costs for goods are a meaningful share of turnover. Service businesses with few purchases are usually classed as limited cost traders and pay 16.5%, which rarely beats standard accounting. We will run both calculations for you.
