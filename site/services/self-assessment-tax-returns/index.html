Self Assessment, done properly and well before January.

If HMRC has asked you for a tax return, or you think they should have, we will prepare it, check it, tell you what you owe, and file it. Most clients send us their paperwork in the summer and have forgotten about it by the autumn.

Who needs to file

Anyone who is self-employed with income over £1,000, a partner in a business, a landlord with rental income, a company director taking dividends, someone earning over £150,000 through PAYE, or anyone with untaxed income from abroad, investments or a side business. You also need to file if your income is over £60,000 and your household receives Child Benefit, because of the High Income Child Benefit Charge.

If you are not sure, ask. Plenty of people are paying penalties for returns they did not know they had to file, and a few are filing returns they could stop.

The dates that matter

  • 5 October — register for Self Assessment if this is your first year
  • 31 January — file the online return and pay the balance, plus the first payment on account for the next year
  • 31 July — second payment on account

A return filed one day late costs £100 even if no tax is owed, and the penalties keep climbing after three, six and twelve months. Interest runs on late payments from 1 February.

What we do

We gather what we need from you, prepare the return, check it for anything missed, and go through it with you before anything is submitted. You will know exactly what the bill is and when it falls due, and if payments on account are going to cause a cash-flow problem we will tell you whether a claim to reduce them is sensible.

For landlords we handle the restriction on mortgage interest relief, the property allowance, and capital gains reporting when you sell, which has to be done within 60 days of completion. For contractors we deal with CIS deductions and getting any overpaid tax refunded. For directors we make sure dividends, salary and benefits all agree with the company's records.

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax begins in April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with gross income over £50,000, and will reach those over £20,000 by April 2028. It replaces the single annual return with quarterly updates from compatible software. If this affects you we will set you up on the right system well ahead of your start date.

Common questions

How much is a tax return?

It depends on what goes in it. A straightforward return for a director or landlord with one property is at the lower end; a sole trader with full accounts, several properties and capital gains is more work. We quote a fixed price after a short conversation, and it will not change unless the job does.

I have missed the deadline. Can you still help?

Yes. The sooner it is filed the less it costs, so get in touch straight away. We can also appeal penalties where there is a reasonable excuse, though HMRC applies that fairly narrowly.

What expenses can I claim as a sole trader?

Anything incurred wholly and exclusively for the business: materials, tools, van costs, insurance, a share of phone and home-office costs, professional fees, and so on. The grey areas are usually mixed-use items like a car or a home office, and that is where good advice pays for itself.

Do I need to send you receipts for everything?

You need to keep them for five years after the filing deadline, but you do not need to post them to us. A tidy spreadsheet or a bookkeeping app with receipts photographed is ideal. We will tell you if we need to see anything specific.
