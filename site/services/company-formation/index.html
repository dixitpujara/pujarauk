Starting a business, set up properly from the first day.

The decisions you make in the first month, such as sole trader or limited, VAT or not yet, which bank and which software, shape how much admin and tax you deal with for years. We help you make them with your eyes open, then do the set-up so you can get on with finding customers.

Sole trader or limited company?

There is no universal answer, and anyone who gives you one without asking questions is guessing. As a rough guide, a limited company starts to make sense when profits are consistently above the higher-rate threshold, when customers or agencies insist on it, when you want to bring in a partner or investor, or when limited liability genuinely matters for the work you do. Below that, the extra accounting and admin of a company often costs more than it saves.

We will look at your expected income, your other income, whether you have a spouse or partner to share ownership with, and what the business is likely to look like in three years, and give you a clear recommendation. You can always incorporate later; it is harder to go back the other way.

What we set up

  • Incorporation at Companies House with sensible share structure and articles, usually within 24 hours
  • Directors' identity verification, which Companies House now requires
  • Registration for corporation tax, PAYE and, if needed, VAT and CIS
  • Registration for Self Assessment if you are going self-employed
  • A registered office address, if you would rather not use your home
  • Bookkeeping software, bank feed and invoice templates
  • A written note of every deadline for your first year and what each one means

The first year

New businesses rarely need much accounting work in year one, but they do need someone to ask. How to pay yourself. Whether to put the van through the business. What to do with that letter from HMRC. Our start-up packages include unlimited questions by phone and email, because the five-minute call that stops a mistake is the most valuable thing we do.

Already set up but not sure it was done right? We see a lot of companies formed through online agents with odd share structures, the wrong SIC code, or a registered office that has since lapsed. A quick review is cheap and usually reassuring.

Common questions

How long does it take to form a company?

Usually the same or next working day once we have your details and the directors have completed identity verification. Bank accounts take longer; allow one to three weeks depending on the bank.

What does it cost to set up a limited company?

The Companies House fee for online incorporation is £50. Our fee for the set-up work is fixed and quoted in advance, and for clients taking an ongoing package we often include it.

Can my spouse be a shareholder?

Often, yes, and it can be tax-efficient, but it needs to be done properly with real shares carrying real rights, not an arrangement that only exists on paper. We will explain the rules and set it up correctly if it suits you.

Do I need a business bank account as a sole trader?

Not legally, but in practice yes. Mixing personal and business money makes bookkeeping slow and makes any HMRC enquiry much more painful. Several banks offer free accounts for the first year.
