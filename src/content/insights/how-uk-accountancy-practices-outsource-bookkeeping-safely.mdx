## Why practices outsource

Most UK practices do not outsource because they want to. They outsource because the recurring work has outgrown the team. Bookkeeping, VAT return preparation, payroll runs and year-end accounts preparation are the jobs that arrive every week, cannot be deferred, and are hardest to recruit for. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax has turned annual clients into quarterly ones. A practice that cannot hire quickly enough ends up with partners doing bank reconciliations at nine in the evening.

An outsourced team takes the preparation work off the practice's desk while the practice keeps the client, the review, the advice and the submission. That division is the whole point, and it is worth being clear about it from the first conversation.

## What to look for in a partner

### Qualifications you can check

Ask who will actually do the work and what they are qualified in. A reputable team will name its principal, state the professional body and give a membership or firm registration number you can verify. If the provider is overseas, the qualification should be described honestly, for example "Chartered Accountants (India), registered with ICAI", rather than borrowing UK titles.

### UK knowledge, not just accounting knowledge

Preparing UK accounts means knowing FRS 102 and FRS 105 formats, the VAT schemes, PAYE and auto-enrolment, Companies House filing formats and the quirks of the software your clients use. Ask for a sample of UK work, and ask what happens when the team is unsure of a UK rule.

### Working hours that match yours

If a query from your team at 10 am gets an answer at 10 am, the work flows. If it gets an answer tomorrow, every job takes a day longer. Insist on a team that works UK hours and gives you a named contact.

### A review process on their side

The partner should have its own two-stage check before anything reaches you: the preparer, then a reviewer. You will still review, but you should be reviewing finished work, not catching basic errors.

### Clear scope and clear pricing

Agree in writing what is included, what is not, the turnaround time for each type of job, and the price basis. Fixed monthly fees per client work well for bookkeeping and VAT; per-job fees work for accounts preparation. Ambiguity about scope is the most common source of friction.

## How the handover works

A good onboarding takes two to four weeks and follows a predictable path.

1. **Scoping call.** You describe the clients, the software, the volumes and the deadlines. The partner confirms what it can take on and what it would rather not.
2. **Agreements.** Engagement letter, confidentiality agreement and the data processing agreement with the transfer safeguards described below.
3. **Access.** The partner is given access to the practice's software (Xero, QuickBooks, Sage, FreeAgent, BrightPay and the like) through its own user accounts, never shared passwords. Access is scoped to the clients being handled.
4. **Pilot.** Two or three clients for one cycle. The practice reviews everything. Both sides agree what to change.
5. **Roll-out.** Clients are added in batches with a working calendar: when records arrive, when the first draft is due, when the review is due, when the deadline falls.
6. **Rhythm.** A short weekly call or written update, and a monthly review of turnaround and quality.

## The risks, and how to manage them

### Data protection

When client data moves to a team outside the UK, the practice is making an international transfer of personal data and remains the controller. For a team in India, which has no UK adequacy decision, the transfer needs a lawful mechanism: the UK International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA) or the UK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, supported by a transfer risk assessment. The practice's privacy notice should tell clients that data may be processed outside the UK. The practice's ICO registration should be current. None of this is difficult, but it has to be done before the first file is shared, not after.

Ask the partner how data is stored, who can see it, how access is removed when a staff member leaves, and whether anything is kept on personal devices. Use a secure document exchange or the software's own portal rather than email attachments.

### Client confidentiality

The partner's staff are bound by their own professional body's code of ethics. Pujara & Co's team is bound by the ICAI Code of Ethics, which carries confidentiality obligations comparable to those of UK bodies. Put the obligation in the contract as well, with a right to audit.

### Anti-money-laundering

The UK anti-money-laundering obligations of a supervised accountancy practice, including client due diligence, risk assessment and reporting, remain with the UK practice. An overseas preparation team does not take over those duties and should not be relied on to perform them. The practice should also be aware of its own supervisory body's rules on outsourcing and on the use of overseas resource, and should tell its supervisor if required.

### Client perception

Some clients care where their work is done; most care that it is done well and on time. Be straightforward: tell clients that the practice uses an outsourced preparation team and that the practice reviews and remains responsible. Hiding it is the only approach that goes wrong.

### Dependency

Keep documentation of every process in the practice's own systems so that the work could be brought back in-house or moved if needed. A partner that resists documenting its process is a red flag.

## Checklist before you sign

- Named principal, professional body and registration number verified
- Sample UK work reviewed by a senior member of your team
- Working hours and named contact confirmed in writing
- Scope, turnaround times and pricing documented
- Data processing agreement with IDTA or Addendum and a transfer risk assessment completed
- Privacy notice updated and ICO registration checked
- Software access set up on individual, scoped accounts
- Secure document exchange agreed; no email attachments for client data
- Confidentiality clause and audit right in the contract
- AML responsibilities confirmed as staying with the practice
- Pilot clients chosen and review criteria agreed
- Exit terms and return or deletion of data agreed
