## The change in one paragraph

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (often shortened to MTD for Income Tax or MTD ITSA) replaces the single annual Self Assessment return with digital record-keeping, quarterly updates sent to HMRC through compatible software, and a year-end submission that finalises the position. It started on 6 April 2026 for the first group of sole traders and landlords, and the net widens in April 2027 and April 2028. If you have self-employment or property income, or you run a practice that looks after people who do, this affects you.

## Who is in, and when

The test is "qualifying income": your gross income from self-employment and property added together, before expenses. Employment income, pensions, dividends and interest do not count towards the test, although they are still reported at the year end.

| From | Qualifying income threshold |
| --- | --- |
| 6 April 2026 | over £50,000 |
| April 2027 | over £30,000 |
| April 2028 | over £20,000 |

HMRC looks at the figures on the Self Assessment return for an earlier tax year to decide whether you cross the line, so a good year some time ago can bring you in now. HMRC writes to people it believes are in scope, but the responsibility to check sits with the taxpayer. The GOV.UK eligibility checker linked at the end of this article is the place to confirm your own position.

A few groups are exempt or deferred, including people who cannot use digital tools for reasons such as disability or location, and certain trusts, estates and partnerships. If you think an exemption applies, you have to apply for it rather than assume it.

## What you have to do differently

### Keep digital records

Every business transaction, whether a sale, an expense or a rental receipt, has to be recorded in software or a spreadsheet that connects to software. Paper ledgers and a shoebox of receipts handed over once a year no longer meet the rule. The record has to be entered digitally, and the link from that record through to the figures sent to HMRC has to be digital too, with no retyping in between.

### Send quarterly updates

Four times a year the software sends HMRC a summary of income and expenses for the quarter. The standard quarters run to 5 July, 5 October, 5 January and 5 April, with each update due by the 7th of the following month. You can elect to use calendar quarters instead if that fits your bookkeeping better. The updates are cumulative totals, not tax calculations, and there is no tax to pay with them. Their purpose is to keep the records current and give HMRC, and you, a running picture.

### Finalise at the year end

After 5 April you confirm the year's figures, add anything that is not in the business records, such as employment income, dividends, pension contributions and reliefs, and make the final declaration through the software. GOV.UK describes this as finalising your tax position, and the payment deadline stays at 31 January after the end of the tax year.

### Use compatible software

HMRC publishes a list of software that works with MTD for Income Tax. Many products are the familiar cloud bookkeeping tools, some are "bridging" tools that connect a spreadsheet to HMRC, and some are built for landlords specifically. Choose on the basis of what you and your accountant can actually operate every quarter, not on the length of the feature list.

## Why the quarterly rhythm matters

The hard part of MTD for Income Tax is not the technology. It is the discipline. A return prepared once a year can absorb months of neglect; a quarterly update cannot. If bank feeds are not reconciled, receipts are not matched and rental statements are not entered by the 7th of the month, the update is wrong or late. Late submissions build up penalty points under the points-based system, and points become fines.

For a practice, that means every MTD client needs a monthly, not annual, bookkeeping cycle. For a landlord with two properties and a full-time job, it means setting aside an hour a month or asking someone to do it.

## Common questions

**Do I have to pay tax quarterly?** No. The updates are information only. Payment dates do not change.

**I have one small rental and a sole trade. Which figure counts?** Both, added together, before expenses.

**Can I still use a spreadsheet?** Yes, if it is digitally linked to bridging software that sends the updates. Copying totals by hand into a return is not allowed.

**What if I joined part-way through a year?** You generally join from the start of a tax year, based on the earlier year's return. Check the GOV.UK guidance for the year that applies to you.

**Can my accountant do all of this for me?** An authorised agent can keep the records, send the updates and make the final declaration on your behalf. You remain responsible for the figures.

## What to do now

- Check your qualifying income for the relevant earlier tax year against the thresholds above.
- If you are in scope, choose software and set up digital records before the first quarter you are required to report, not after.
- Agree who does what each quarter: who enters records, who reviews, who submits.
- If you run a practice, list your clients by threshold year and plan the capacity you will need for 2027 and 2028.
